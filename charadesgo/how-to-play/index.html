How to play charades

The classic rules, the hand signals everyone uses, and how to keep score.

Charades is a guessing game where one player acts out a word or title without speaking while their team tries to guess it. You need at least three people, something to write on, and a timer. That’s it.

What you need

  • 3 or more players (6–12 is the sweet spot)
  • Slips of paper and a bowl or hat, or a charades app
  • A timer (a phone works fine)

Classic charades rules

  1. Split into two teams. Try to mix ages and loud/quiet players evenly.
  2. Each team writes clues for the other team. Movie, book, song and TV titles are traditional. Fold them and put them in the other team’s bowl.
  3. One player draws a slip and has a few seconds to read it silently.
  4. Start the timer. Two minutes per turn is standard; go to three for younger kids.
  5. Act it out with no talking. No sounds, no mouthing words, no spelling letters in the air, and no pointing at objects in the room that give the answer away.
  6. The team guesses out loud. If they get it before time runs out, they score a point.
  7. Teams alternate until everyone has acted at least once. Most points wins.

Charades hand signals

These gestures are what make charades charades. Agree on them before you start so nobody argues later.

What kind of title is it?

  • Movie: crank an old-fashioned film camera beside your head.
  • Book: press your palms together, then open them like a book.
  • Song: open your mouth wide and spread your arms like you’re singing opera.
  • TV show: draw a rectangle in the air with both index fingers.
  • Play or musical: pull an imaginary rope to open stage curtains.
  • Quote or phrase: make air quotes with two fingers on each hand.

How many words?

  • Number of words: hold up that many fingers.
  • Which word you’re acting: hold up fingers again (two fingers = second word).
  • Number of syllables: lay that many fingers on your forearm.
  • Which syllable: lay fingers on your forearm again.
  • The whole thing at once: sweep your arms in a big circle.

Helpful shortcuts

  • “Sounds like”: cup a hand behind your ear, then act a rhyming word.
  • “The”: make a T with your index fingers.
  • Short word (a, an, of, in): hold your thumb and index finger close together.
  • Longer / plural: pull your hands apart like stretching taffy.
  • Shorter: push your hands together, or make a chopping motion.
  • “Yes, exactly!”: touch your nose and point at the person who said it.
  • “Close, keep going”: beckon with both hands.

The phone version (heads-up style)

Most families now play a faster version with a phone. Instead of acting silently, the guesser holds the phone to their forehead and everyone else gives clues any way they like: talking, acting, singing, impressions.

  1. Pick a deck: movies, animals, kids words, holiday cards, anything.
  2. The guesser holds the phone to their forehead, screen facing out.
  3. Everyone else shouts clues at the same time.
  4. Tilt the phone down for a correct guess, up to pass.
  5. When the round ends, see your score and pass the phone.

Tips for a better game

  • Start easy. The first round should be quick wins so shy players warm up.
  • Pair little kids with a grown-up who whispers the word and helps them act it.
  • Ban one rule-bender at a time. If someone keeps mouthing words, give that team a one-point penalty.
  • Theme the night. Holiday charades, Disney night, or decade-themed rounds keep it fresh.

Fun variations

  • Reverse charades: the whole team acts while one person guesses.
  • Speed round: 60 seconds, as many words as possible.
  • Charades relay: one player sees the word and acts it to a teammate, who acts it to the next, telephone style. The last person guesses.
  • Sound effects only: no gestures, just noises. Great with the animals deck.
